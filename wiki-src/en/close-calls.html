Close call ({{ t('GRAZE') }})

{{ img('graze', 'Lesson 1-03: the head runs along its own body; every new body cell beside it is a close call.') }}

After each step the game looks at the four cells around the head (up, down, left, right — not diagonals). Each one that holds a conductor is a close call:

  • Your own body, but only its older part: the {{ GameSim.MIN_AGE }} cells right behind the head (the neck and a tight hairpin) don't count, and neither does the very tip of the tail that is about to move away. You have to actually run alongside your body.
  • Structures: Campaign insulators, gate pylons, live relay posts and bridge bars, live wires. They kill on contact but are good for close calls.

Rules that keep it fair:

  • Each body cell pays once. After that it shows a thin spark line inside (it is "charged") and never pays again.
  • A cell of the board that just produced a close call can't produce another for {{ GameSim.GRAZE_COOLDOWN }} steps — this stops endless loops chasing your own tail.
  • An insulator pays, goes dark, and lights up again after {{ GameSim.WALL_REARM }} steps (below).

This charge from close calls is "minor": the higher the multiplier, the less of it you keep — {{ pct(GameSim.FLOW_MINOR_TAPER[0]) }} at ×1, {{ pct(GameSim.FLOW_MINOR_TAPER[2]) }} at ×3, only {{ pct(GameSim.FLOW_MINOR_TAPER[4]) }} at ×5. Close calls alone top out around ×3; higher stages need maneuvers. Inside a hot zone every close call charges in full.

Tight steps and maneuvers

A tight step is a step where both side cells of the head (left and right of the direction of travel) hold a conductor: you are in a one-cell gap. Each tight step with a fresh side adds {{ GameSim.FLOW_TIGHT }} charge, and the meter does not drain while you are inside a gap.

When the head leaves the gap, the run of tight steps is judged:

So a one-step gap is a THREAD only if it really goes through a wall of your body; a short gap with an easy way round is a SQUEEZE (two steps or more) or nothing (one step).

Fresh sides only

A gap only pays if at least one of its sides is fresh body, never used as a side of a gap before. Driving the same corridor again pays nothing. A gap made only of pylons is not a maneuver either — the gate pays for it.

Chains

A maneuver less than {{ GameSim.CHAIN_WINDOW }} steps after the previous one continues a chain (shown as {{ t('CHAIN %d').replace('%d', 'N') }}). The chain multiplies both points and charge of the maneuver:

{{ table(['Maneuver in the chain', '1st', '2nd', '3rd', '4th', '5th and on'], [['Bonus', '×1', '×1.25', '×1.5', '×1.75', '×2']], 'num') }}

The chain breaks when {{ GameSim.CHAIN_WINDOW }} steps pass without a maneuver or the meter runs empty.

Example

A THREAD through a 1-cell gap at ×3, second in a chain: (60 + 4 × 1) × 1.25 = 80 base points, × 3 = {{ (GameSim.PTS_THREAD + GameSim.PTS_TIGHT) * 5 // 4 * 3 }} points, and {{ GameSim.FLOW_THREAD * 5 // 4 }} charge.

Crashing in a gap pays nothing for it: the run ends with THREAD or SQUEEZE failed.

Insulators

{{ img('insulator', 'Lesson 1-06: lit blocks on the board; one just brushed has gone dark.') }}

Campaign boards can have fixed blocks, insulators. Hitting one ends the run. Brushing one is a close call that is always paid in full (it is not your body, so hunger does not apply). After a close call the block goes dark and re-arms after {{ GameSim.WALL_REARM }} steps. Insulators also work as sides of a gap: a SQUEEZE between your body and a row of blocks counts.
